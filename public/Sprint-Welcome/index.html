Seasoned Designer and Manager

Creating a user centered design culture

My first job out of school was at a technology company called Novell. I was one of several contractors who were brought on to replace the previous web team. Kim Eaves, the new director over the web team was stuck working with a mix of employees and three separate vendors. Despite this, we were able to work together as one cohesive team. We formed a core leadership team that included all the core skill sets (development, design, project management, content). Together we made a real difference at Novell.

I realized right way that we needed objective data not only to help inform design decisions but to make stakeholder presentations more productive. My team of designers and I approached our work from the perspective of the user. We started with simple user flow charts then worked up to full personas. We got training in usability research and went from a design and reveal approach to an inclusive iterative design process informed by usability testing.

Our design team grew from 2 designers in 2002 to 7 by the end of 2010. We contributed to Novell's website becoming the primary source for new business leads for the company.

Here are a few highlights from my 9 years at Novell.

  • Improving novell.com lead generation from 14% to 60% of all leads at Novell
  • Designing a global website that had localized content for 15 countries and 13 languages
  • Facilitating cross team collaboration and support for user centric design

Naturally curious, complex problem solver

I was “5 whys” before design thinking made it popular

There is a saying in Germany that goes like this “he who knows not and knows that he knows not knows more than he who knows not and knows not that he knows not” Kind of a brain twister but this saying reflects the approach I have taken in life. Head nodding and buzzword dropping to appear smart may be the easiest path in the short term but it does not grow knowledge for you and those around you. I love approaching a complex challenge by pushing past the assumptions and making sure I and those around me understand the core issues, motivations, and objectives for any given problem.

By clearly understanding what we do and don’t know, we can make a plan to go find the answers. This all leads to better solutions. Better for the business and better for the users.

Thinking back over the years these are some of the projects where my approach has made the biggest difference.

  • Asurion - “Speed Claims” A new approach to the online claims process that found the sweet spot between the users desire for a quick easy process and the businesses very real need to manage fraud and complex backend processes.
  • Asurion - Small business offering. Guided the team to define an MVP that meet the business needs while keeping the features inline with user needs.
  • Asurion - Facilitated the adaptation of design thinking and the design sprint process for organizational structure and process design.
  • Novell - Intranet consolidation. Managed to get a siloed organization to put on a unified face for the sake of the employees across the company.
  • Novell - Product page redesign 2010. Leveraged user needs identified through research to inform and prioritize content.
  • Eli Kirk - Selling discovery and usability research in a small agency. Facilitated getting basic discovery included in the scoping phase for small budget projects. Offered an inexpensive “UX Audit” to get clients hooked on research.
